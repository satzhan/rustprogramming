Some field notes

The best part of a hard problem
is usually the part
where you're still stuck.

These are some of the things people actually do when they work a problem out. You're probably doing half of them already without thinking about it. The rest are here in case they turn out to be useful to you. None of it is a rule.

Here's something worth knowing early. Almost nobody reads a problem, sees the answer, and just types it in. What really happens is messier: you read it, you try the first thing that comes to mind, it doesn't quite work, and then you figure out why. That “why” is usually where the understanding actually shows up. The four parts below follow the shape you already work in: read it, try it, see how other people did it, then look back. Each one has a couple of extra things you can do. Read it on your own, or use it to compare notes with someone who solved the same problem a different way.

How an idea travels

One idea, three problems that don't look alike

Here's the nice thing. An idea you pick up on one problem doesn't stay put. Take this one: keep track of what you've already seen, so you never have to go back and look at it again. It quietly turns up in three problems that look completely unrelated at first.

Find two that sum to a target

2711

Instead of checking every pair, jot down the numbers you've already seen. One pass through.

Biggest rise in a sequence

545065

Don't compare every pair. Just hang onto the smallest value you've seen so far.

Matching brackets

([]

Keep whatever's still open in a small pile. The most recent one is what a closing bracket has to answer to.

Same idea every time: spend a little memory so you can throw away a whole loop. The thing worth keeping was never the answer to any one of these problems. It's the idea itself, which you can carry straight to the next one.

One last thing

Pick one.

Anything you marked “I'll try this” collects here. You don't need all of them, and honestly you shouldn't try. Pick one to carry into your next problem and click it to star it. One change you actually use beats a long list you don't.
